## Pricing Math (basic model)
For each repair type: **Price = Parts Cost + Labor Value + Margin**
- Track actual parts cost per job (from supplier invoices)
- Set a minimum margin target per job (e.g. 40–50% gross margin) — refine once real numbers come in
- Don't race-to-the-bottom on price against big chains; compete on trust, turnaround, and taking on jobs others reject (motherboard work)

## GST Registration (Australia)
- GST registration is **mandatory once turnover reaches $75,000/year** (or is expected to) — below that, it's optional.
- Decision: register now (adds credibility, lets you claim GST credits on tool/parts purchases, but adds BAS reporting obligation) vs. wait until closer to threshold.
- **Action:** decide and record here once confirmed with a bookkeeper/accountant or the ATO.

## Bookkeeping Basics
- Open a **separate business bank account** immediately — never mix personal and business funds, even as a sole operator
- Track every expense with a receipt (parts, tools, fuel for pickups, subscriptions)
- Simple spreadsheet or lightweight accounting software (e.g. free tier tools) is enough at this volume
- Set aside a fixed % of each job's revenue for tax as you go (don't spend it — avoid a tax-time shock)

## Reinvestment Plan
- Phase 1 profit priority: build a cash buffer that can fund the Phase 2 software house build (dev tools, hosting, possibly first hires) without depending on repair revenue to survive
- Secondary: reinvest in parts inventory breadth (more device models covered = more jobs accepted) and better diagnostic tools — but don't over-invest in the repair business past what Phase 1 revenue goals need, since it's not the long-term focus

## Metrics to Track Monthly
- Revenue, total expenses, net profit
- Average job value and margin
- Cash reserve / runway
